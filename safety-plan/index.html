What a safety plan is

A safety plan is a short, written list of things to do and people to reach if a crisis starts. You make it ahead of time, at a calmer moment, so you don't have to work it out during a hard one.

The Suicide Prevention Resource Center (SPRC) describes it as a written list of ways to cope and sources of support, in the order you would use them. The National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) says a safety plan can help lower a person's risk of acting on thoughts of suicide.

A therapist, counselor, or doctor can help you make one. The American Foundation for Suicide Prevention (AFSP) says a mental health professional can help you build a plan. A plan does not replace professional care. See what the research shows about safety plans.

You don't have to finish a plan before asking for help. You can call or text 988 at any step, and in immediate danger, call 911.

The usual steps

Most safety plans go through the same steps, from things you can do on your own to people who can help. These are the steps the developers of the Stanley-Brown Safety Plan and AFSP describe, in Tidelight's own words.

  1. Notice your warning signs

    Write down the thoughts, feelings, or situations that tend to come before a hard time for you. Spotting them early tells you it's time to use the rest of the plan.

  2. Things that help, on your own

    List things you can do by yourself to get through a hard moment, without needing anyone else there. Only you know what works for you. It might be music, a walk, a shower, or something that keeps your hands busy.

  3. People and places that take your mind off things

    Write down people you like being around, and places where you feel a little better, such as a friend's home, a park, or a library. You don't have to talk about how you feel with them.

  4. People you can ask for help

    List family members or friends you can tell that you're struggling, with their phone numbers. If you're under 18, it can help to include an adult you trust, such as a relative, teacher, coach, or school counselor. If there's no adult you feel safe telling, you can still call or text 988 yourself.

  5. Professionals and crisis lines

    Write down your doctor, therapist, or counselor, if you have one. Add the 988 Lifeline: you can call or text 988 any time. In immediate danger, call 911.

  6. Making your space safer

    Think about how to keep things you could use to hurt yourself away from you during a hard time. You might ask someone you trust to keep them, or to help you store them safely. A doctor, a counselor, or 988 can help you work out what makes sense for you.

NIMH says research supports storing dangerous items safely to help prevent suicide. See what the research shows about safer surroundings.

Making and keeping your plan

  • Make it at a calmer time, on your own or with someone you trust. A professional can help, too.
  • Keep it where you can reach it quickly. On paper, other people could read it, so choose a place that feels safe to you.
  • Share it if you want to. Some people give a copy to someone in step 4, so that person knows how to help.
  • Change it when things change, such as a new phone number, a new place that helps, or a new doctor.

This page saves nothing and has nothing to type. Tidelight never sees your plan. The developers of the Stanley-Brown Safety Plan also offer their own form, linked on Get Help.

A blank plan to print

Print it, then fill it in by hand. Only the plan prints, on one or two pages, with 988 and 911 on it. You can also copy the steps onto any piece of paper.

If nothing happens, open this page in your web browser and print from its Share button or menu.

About Tidelight

Tidelight is an early project to help people in the United States find suicide prevention support. Tidelight is not a crisis service. The call, text, and chat links on this site connect you to outside services, such as the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 911.

Privacy: this site has no sign-ups, forms, analytics, or cookies. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub says it logs and stores visitors' IP addresses for security. Quick exit and saved offline copies have limits. Read about privacy and what this site can't hide.
