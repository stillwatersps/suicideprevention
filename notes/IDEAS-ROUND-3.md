# Forty ideas to make Tidelight the best it can be

[[00 START HERE|Back to Start Here]]

One founder asked, on November 9, 2026, for thirty things that would make this the best app of its
kind, with two of their own worked out in full, and then ten more. Claude wrote this list after
reading every note and page on the branch. **Each one is a proposal.** Mark it Approved, Not now,
or No, with who agreed and the date, and move approved ones to [[TASKS]]. Anything that adds
health or crisis wording also needs the licensed reviewer, and most of the app ideas need a lawyer
to read the privacy side. The earlier lists are in [[IDEAS]]; nothing here repeats what is built.

"Best" here means: the fastest route to real help, honest about what it is, safe on a shared phone,
usable by a teen and a grandparent, and never a substitute for a person. Every idea was checked
against [[SAFETY]]: no risk scores, no reading what people write, no AI chat, nothing sent without
a tap.

## The two worked out in full

| # | Idea | Where it's worked out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F3 | **Alert people I choose when I stay near a hard place.** The founder's idea: share a location with a group, and when the phone notices the person has been near such a place for a set time, alert the group with the location | [[LOCATION-ALERT]]: step by step, what phones allow, where the places come from, who gets the alert, risks, and the order to build in. Short version: build it on the person's own places (E1), with a one-tap send, and no built-in list |
| F4 | **Four daily check-ins**, morning, midday, after school if wanted, and before bed, **with the option to share them with a guardian or a doctor** | [[CHECK-IN-SCHEDULE]]. The part that needs no server is built as a draft on branch `app/daily-check-ins`: four optional reminders, today's check-ins listed, and Share today or Share the last 7 days through the phone's share sheet |

## Thirty things (F1 to F30)

### Reaching help faster

| # | Idea | Why it matters | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | **A personal safety plan in the app**: warning signs, what helps, people, places, professionals, and reasons for living, in the order clinicians use, made with a counselor, printable, with 988 on every copy | Safety planning has the strongest evidence of anything an app can hold ([[AGE-STRATEGIES]]). The Get Help page describes one; the app can keep one | Reviewer approves the structure and words; kept on the phone only |
| F2 | **My people**: up to three chosen contacts, callable or textable in one tap from every screen of the app, next to 988 | Most people in a hard moment reach a person they know first. The tap should be as short as the one for 988 | Contacts stay on the phone. Reviewer on the wording |
| F3 | Alert people I choose when I stay near a hard place | See above | [[LOCATION-ALERT]] |
| F4 | Four daily check-ins, shared on purpose | See above | [[CHECK-IN-SCHEDULE]] |
| F5 | **Home-screen and lock-screen widgets**: Call 988, Text 988, and Call 911 without opening the app, plus a Siri and Google Assistant phrase that opens the crisis screen | Fewer taps when hands shake. Widgets work with the phone locked | Native code per platform; test on real phones; never a test call ([[SAFETY]] rule 7) |
| F6 | **While you wait**: when someone opens 988 chat or is on hold, the app shows what to expect, step by step, and one grounding exercise | Waiting is when people give up. 988's own "what to expect" pages exist; the recorded menu still needs a person to confirm ([[SAFETY]]) | Reviewer; a person confirms the 988 steps |
| F7 | **Local help by state**: state crisis lines, mobile crisis teams, and groups such as the Native and Strong Lifeline, chosen by the person's state, never by location tracking | 988 is national; many people also want someone local. Today the site has only national services | A person reads and re-checks every entry; the monthly link check covers it; upkeep owner named in [[UPKEEP]] |
| F8 | **Making home safer**: a reviewed page on putting time and distance between a person and the means of harm, for families | Means-safety counseling is among the best-supported prevention steps. Nothing on the site covers it, and it must be written with care | Reviewer writes or approves it; safe messaging rules apply ([[SAFETY]] rule 4) |

### Staying well between hard moments

| # | Idea | Why it matters | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F9 | **Hope box**: photos, a voice note, a message from someone, a song title, kept on the phone and shown from the safety plan | Reasons for living are more persuasive in a person's own words and pictures than in ours | On-phone only; part of F1 |
| F10 | **Grounding and breathing exercises**, two or three, short, with no sound needed | [[FIRST-VERSION]] has none yet, and the Not sure page already points to feelings. People ask for something to do right now | Reviewer chooses which; nothing that could be used as a substitute for help |
| F11 | **A weekly look back**, with no scores: how many days checked in, things enjoyed, reflections kept, one line the person wrote | Shows change over time without a mood graph. Graphs invite scoring; a list of good things doesn't | Built on the saved check-ins in the app |
| F12 | **Caring notes to yourself**: the person writes notes on a good day; the app shows one, at a time they chose, on later days | Modeled on "caring contacts" letters, which cut suicide attempts after hospital care in studies summarized in [[AGE-STRATEGIES]]. | F13 | **Recovery stories** from people with lived experience, with consent, edited to safe-messaging rules (idea D4) | Hope with a face. Safe-messaging guidance recommends them | A consent process, the reviewer, and someone with lived experience editing |
| F14 | **After the hospital or the ER**: a reviewed page for the weeks after a crisis visit, for the person and their family | The weeks after discharge carry the highest risk, and the site says nothing about them | Reviewer; links to 988's follow-up services |
| F15 | **Sleep and the before-bed check-in**: the last check-in of the day can offer a short wind-down and a note on sleep | Sleep problems are a known risk factor, and "before bed" is already one of the four times | Reviewer |

### Honest, private, and accessible

| # | Idea | Why it matters | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F21 | **App lock**: Face ID, fingerprint, or a code, on by choice | Anyone who can unlock the phone can read the journal today. A teen at home needs this most | Native code; a lawyer on the wording |
| F22 | **A plain app name and icon, by choice** | On some phones, "Tidelight" on the home screen is itself a risk. Some domestic-violence apps offer a plain look | Reviewer and a lawyer first: the stores' rules, and whether hiding is right for minors |
| F23 | **Export my data** as one readable file, next to Delete everything | Control over one's own words. Also lets someone bring a journal to a therapist | On-phone; share sheet |
| F24 | **Accessibility pass**: VoiceOver and TalkBack on every screen, dynamic type, reduced motion, switch control, and a Deaf reviewer for the Deaf section | The site aims at AAA contrast but no screen-reader user or Deaf reviewer has tried it | People to test; fixes |
| F25 | **More languages**, starting with the next most spoken where the site launches, each with its own fluent reviewer, and 988's interpreter line explained on every page | 988 answers in 240 languages by phone; the site speaks two | Fluent reviewers; the packets already support a language |
| F26 | **Simple words mode**: shorter sentences and bigger type, chosen by the reader, on the crisis pages first | Readers in distress, young readers, and readers of English as a second language. `check_readability.py` shows where it's needed | Reviewer approves the simpler text as new wording |
| F27 | **A "last checked" line on every crisis number**, filled by the monthly check and a person's confirmation | Trust. Today the About page says nothing has been confirmed by a person, which is true, and it should change the moment it isn't | `set_checked_date.py` exists; a person confirms |
| F28 | **A privacy page in plain words with an outside review** of the app's claims (what's on the phone, what's in backups, what Tidelight can see: nothing) | The app's promise is its main advantage. A reviewed promise is worth more | A lawyer or a privacy reviewer |

## Not proposed, and why

- **Risk scores, mood graphs, or any reading of what people write**, including by AI. [[SAFETY]]
  rule 5 and the founder's spec. A graph invites a score.
- **An AI chat or companion.** Tidelight points to people. The reviewer's question list in
  [[CONTENT-REVIEW]] explains the limits.
- **A public community or peer forum.** Nobody is watching it at 3 a.m., and moderation is a job.
- **A built-in list of places known for suicide.** [[LOCATION-ALERT]] explains.
- **Automatic alerts or sharing without a tap**, until both founders choose accounts and a server
  with eyes open ([[CHECK-IN-BACKEND]]).
