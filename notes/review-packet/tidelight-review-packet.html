How to use this packet

What's inside:

  • Part 1: Text that appears on every page
  • Part 2: Each English page, sentence by sentence, with its source links
  • Part 3: The founder-supplied research brief
  • Part 4: Crisis resources, and what each button does
  • Part 5: Questions written for you
  • Part 6: How the research was read, and how the pages compare with the brief
  • Part 7: Proposed shorter sentences, optional
  • Part 8: Decisions and sign-off

The Spanish pages are in a separate packet for a fluent reader.

How to record feedback:

  • Mark each section. Each section ends with a box: OK as written, OK with changes, or Not for the website.
  • Refer to sentences by number. Every sentence has a number, such as HELP-12. In the Notes line, write something like "HELP-12: change to ...". Writing on the page is fine.
  • Repeated sentences. A gray sentence marked "same as" repeats an earlier one. A decision on the first applies to all of them.
  • Answer the questions in Part 5 if you can. Skip any that aren't yours to answer.
  • Finish with Part 8. It records your decisions, your role, and the date. Please don't write personal contact details in this packet. It may be stored in a public project.

You can use a pen on paper, or add notes to the PDF in a PDF app. Return it to the founder who gave it to you.

If anything here could cause harm, please say so first. Use this box, or tell the founders right away, for anything that should come down or change before anything else happens.

You can see the draft website as it looks on a screen if a founder shares a preview link. The draft pages are not on the public website yet. Parts of an older version are public at https://stillwatersps.github.io/suicideprevention/.

BRIEF-6 1. Understanding Suicide and Suicidal Behavior

BRIEF-7 Definitions.

BRIEF-8 Suicide is a death resulting from intentional self-directed behavior.

BRIEF-9 Suicidal ideation means thoughts about suicide; a suicide attempt is nonfatal suicidal behavior.

BRIEF-10 Suicide prevention includes interventions that reduce risk and strengthen access to care.

BRIEF-11 Public health perspective.

BRIEF-12 Suicide is influenced by overlapping individual, interpersonal, community and societal factors.

BRIEF-13 No one diagnosis or experience explains every case.

BRIEF-14 Myths and accurate framing.

BRIEF-15 Depression is one risk factor, not a universal cause.

BRIEF-16 Asking someone directly and compassionately about suicide does not implant the idea.

BRIEF-17 Prevention and recovery are possible.

BRIEF-18 Use nonjudgmental language such as "died by suicide."

Decision: OK as written OK with changes Not for the website Notes:

BRIEF-19 2. Causes and Risk Factors

BRIEF-20 Psychological factors.

BRIEF-21 Depression, anxiety, trauma-related symptoms, substance use disorders and other mental health difficulties may contribute to vulnerability.

BRIEF-22 A diagnosis alone does not predict an individual outcome.

BRIEF-23 Biological factors.

BRIEF-24 Research investigates genetic vulnerability, stress-response systems, sleep disturbance and emotional regulation.

BRIEF-25 None is a deterministic explanation.

BRIEF-26 Social and environmental factors.

BRIEF-27 Bullying, discrimination, interpersonal conflict, loss, economic instability, violence, social isolation and limited access to care can add stress.

BRIEF-28 Risk factors can accumulate or interact.

BRIEF-29 Protective factors.

BRIEF-30 Supportive relationships, belonging, accessible treatment, coping skills, stable environments and continuity of care can reduce risk.

BRIEF-31 Protective factors help but do not guarantee safety.

BRIEF-32 3. Warning Signs and Recognizing Distress

BRIEF-33 Emotional and behavioral indicators.

BRIEF-34 Potential concerns include persistent hopelessness, feeling trapped, intense shame, withdrawal, significant changes in sleep or functioning, agitation, and direct statements about suicide.

BRIEF-35 Warning signs are not a diagnosis.

BRIEF-36 Responding.

BRIEF-37 Listen calmly and without judgment.

BRIEF-38 Ask clearly about suicide when concerned.

BRIEF-39 Do not dismiss statements as attention-seeking or promise secrecy where safety is at risk.

BRIEF-40 Involve qualified help and a trusted adult for young people.

BRIEF-41 Urgent support.

BRIEF-42 In the United States, call or text 988 for crisis support.

BRIEF-43 For immediate life-threatening danger, contact emergency services.

BRIEF-56 Three levels.

BRIEF-57 Primary prevention reduces upstream risks through connectedness, education and supportive environments.

BRIEF-58 Secondary prevention recognizes emerging concerns and connects people to care.

BRIEF-59 Tertiary prevention emphasizes recovery, follow-up and continuity of treatment.
