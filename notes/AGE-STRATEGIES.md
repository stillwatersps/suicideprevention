# What helps at which age: research for idea E3

[[00 START HERE|Back to Start Here]]

Written November 9, 2026, at one founder's request, for idea E3 in [[IDEAS]]: date of birth at
sign-up in the phone app, and help shaped by age group. **Claude's summary. No licensed reviewer
has read it.** The reviewer decides the age groups and what each one gets. Nothing here is advice
for a person, and none of it replaces 988, 911, or care from a professional.

## How this was read

Claude read each source below on November 9, 2026, from the publisher's or a library's own page,
and quotes it. Where a page could not be read, it is listed under "Not read" and nothing is taken
from it. Method details are left out on purpose, as [[SAFETY]] asks; "access to lethal means" is
used instead. Quotes leave out reference numbers.

A caution that runs through all of it: most studies are about clinics, schools, and health
systems, not apps. They say what helps people of an age, not what an app should do. The app's job
is to point people toward the things that work, and to never stand in for them.

## What the sources say, by age

### Every age

- **Ask, listen, connect, follow up.** NIMH's five steps: "ASK: 'Are you thinking about
  suicide?'"; "BE THERE"; "HELP KEEP THEM SAFE"; "HELP THEM CONNECT" with 988 and "a trusted
  family member, friend, spiritual advisor, or mental health professional"; "FOLLOW UP". It says
  "Studies show that asking people if they are suicidal does not increase suicidal behavior or
  thoughts."
- **Staying in touch after a crisis helps.** Mann 2021: "active outreach of psychiatric patients
  after discharge or a suicidal crisis, prevents suicidal behavior", and follow-up as simple as
  postcards prevented attempts in 2 of 4 studies.
- **Limiting access to lethal means saves lives.** Mann 2021: "Means restriction ... prevents
  suicide." NIMH: "making lethal means less available or less deadly can help the person stay
  safe."
- **Therapy works.** Mann 2021: "CBT and DBT prevent suicidal behavior."
- **Self-guided digital tools help a little, if they address suicidal thoughts directly.**
  Torok 2020, 16 trials, 4,398 people aged about 15 to 43 on average: a small effect on suicidal
  thoughts right after use (g −0.18), from tools that target suicidal thoughts directly (g −0.23).
  Tools aimed only at depression were not significant (g −0.12, 95% CI −0.25 to 0.01). Few studies
  followed people beyond 3 months.

### Children, under 13

- **The least studied group.** Mann 2021 names "demographic groups like children and the
  elderly" as understudied. Albaum 2025's studies had a mean age of 15; few children were included.
- **For the app:** nothing here supports self-guided suicide-related features for young
  children. What the sources support is adults around a child: asking, listening, keeping lethal
  means away, and connecting to help (NIMH). So the child group should mainly point to a trusted
  adult, with crisis help always visible. **The reviewer and a lawyer decide** whether children use
  the app at all, and with which adult.

### Teens, 13 to 17

- **Teaching teens themselves works better than training adults around them.** Mann 2021: "Targeting
  high school students prevented student suicide attempts whereas targeting teachers and all but
  one parent study, did not find benefit." Youth Aware of Mental Health (YAM), taught to students,
  "prevented suicidal behavior", where teacher training and screening didn't.
- **A safety plan alone hasn't been shown to help teens.** Albaum 2025, 10 studies and 1,002
  teens (mean age 15): "no significant association between safety planning interventions and
  suicide ideation ... behavior ... attempts", and "available data do not support safety
  planning as a standalone treatment" for young people. Most studies were small, and the authors
  ask for better trials. They suggest "parent or family involvement may be an important
  modification".
- **Training gatekeepers for youth hasn't worked.** Mann 2021: "Education of gatekeepers about
  youth suicidal behavior lacks effectiveness."
- **For the app:** emphasize understanding feelings and mental health, and reaching a trusted
  adult or family member. Offer a safety plan only as something to make with a trusted adult or
  a professional, not as the help itself. Keep The Trevor Project and 988 visible as on the
  website.

### Young adults, 18 to 24

- **Little is proven for students.** Cochrane (Harrod): classroom instruction and gatekeeper
  training raise short-term knowledge and confidence, but "The effect of gatekeeper training on
  suicide or suicidal behavior has not been evaluated." One campus policy limiting access to lethal
  means and requiring professional assessment "significantly reduced student suicides", in one
  study that hasn't been repeated. Evidence is to June 2011, so it is old.
- **For the app:** the "every age" points apply: a safety plan made with someone, reaching out to
  people, therapy. Mention campus counseling and health services as places to start, without
  promising what they offer.

### Adults, 25 to 64

- **Safety planning has the best evidence here.** Nuij 2021, 6 studies, 3,536 people: suicidal
  behaviour fell (relative risk 0.570, 95% CI 0.408–0.795; number needed to treat, 16), but "No
  significant effect was found for suicidal ideation." Albaum 2025 also notes "Evidence supports
  safety planning interventions for adults".
- **Doctors trained in depression lower suicide.** Mann 2021: "Training primary care physicians
  in depression recognition and treatment prevents suicide."
- **For the app:** a personal safety plan (warning signs, ways to cope, people and places,
  professionals to contact, making the surroundings safer, reasons for living), shown to be most
  useful when made or reviewed with a clinician. Encourage talking to a doctor about mood.

### Older adults, 60 or 65 and over

- **Few good studies, and older adults have the highest rates.** Laflamme 2022: "Older people have
  the highest rates of suicide, yet the evidence base on effective suicide preventions in
  late-life is limited." Only four reviews qualified.
- **Collaborative care in primary care, and physical activity.** Laflamme 2022: collaborative care
  "demonstrated significantly less attempts/ideation (OR = 0.80, 95% CI 0.68–0.94)", and
  "Evidence to date supports physical activity and collaborative management for reduction of
  suicide ideation". Antidepressant evidence was "inconclusive", so monitoring is needed when they
  start, change, or stop.
- **Community programs helped women more than men** in Japanese regional studies Mann 2021 lists:
  lower female suicide rates, "No regional difference in male suicides."
- Laflamme 2022 also says older people are "less likely to disclose emotional distress."
- **For the app:** talking to a primary care doctor about mood, staying active, staying in touch
  with people, and plain, larger text. Don't rely on the person bringing up distress themselves.

## A proposal for the app, for the reviewer to change

Proposed by Claude, not agreed by either founder or the reviewer. These are the groups idea E3
listed as a starting point.

| Group | What the app could lead with | What every group still gets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 13 | Telling a trusted adult; a grown-up's guide to asking and listening (NIMH steps) | 988, 911, Quick exit, on every screen |
| 13 to 17 | Understanding feelings, reaching a trusted adult or family member, making a plan with an adult | Same |
| 18 to 24 | Reaching out to people, a safety plan made with someone, campus or local counseling | Same |
| 25 to 64 | A personal safety plan, ideally made or reviewed with a clinician; talking to a doctor | Same |
| 65 and over | Talking to a primary care doctor about mood, staying active, staying in touch | Same |

Every group: no scores, no reading of what's written, and nothing that replaces care.
