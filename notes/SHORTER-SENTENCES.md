# Proposed shorter sentences

[[00 START HERE|Back to Start Here]]

`tools/check_readability.py` lists sentences over 25 words. An AI assistant drafted these shorter versions, and a
second AI session checked that each keeps its limits, numbers, sources, and warnings. **No qualified person has
reviewed them, and the pages have not changed.** Safety wording matters more than shortness, so the reviewer may reject
any of them. If one is accepted, change the page, its Spanish twin, and any page that repeats the sentence.


## Get Help

- **Now (28 words):** The Lifeline's confidentiality statement lists when information can be shared, including when someone is at imminent risk of harm, when the law requires it, or when you agree.
  **Proposed:** The Lifeline's confidentiality statement lists the times when information can be shared. These include when someone is at imminent risk of harm, when the law requires it, or when you agree.
  **For the reviewer:** Keep the link on "confidentiality statement". "These include" keeps the list open, as "including" does: the statement also names other cases, such as court orders. Reviewer's choice: "imminent risk of harm" is the statement's own term. The proposal uses "right now or very soon", which is how this page already words the same idea under emergency services, but a reader could take "very soon" more broadly. To keep the exact term, write "at imminent risk of harm", which also stays under 25 words. The tool's rough estimate is still about grade 9, because long words like "confidentiality" stay. The Spanish page has a matching 37-word sentence.

- **Now (26 words):** On a shared phone or computer, visits to this site can show up in browser history, and calls or texts can show up in phone logs.
  **Proposed:** On a shared phone or computer, visits to this site can show up in browser history. Calls or texts can also show up in phone logs.
  **For the reviewer:** the same sentence is on teens/index.html, so change both or neither. "Also" keeps the second sentence under "On a shared phone or computer". about/index.html has a similar 20-word sentence that the tool doesn't flag. The Spanish page has a matching 32-word sentence.

- **Now (39 words):** The Lifeline recommends that counselors contact emergency services, which can mean 911, police, or a sheriff, only if someone is in danger of being hurt right now or very soon and they can't work out a safer plan together.
  **Proposed:** Emergency services can mean 911, police, or a sheriff. The Lifeline recommends that counselors contact them only if two things are true. First, someone is in danger of being hurt right now or very soon. Second, the counselor and the person who reached out can't work out a safer plan together.
  **For the reviewer:** Keeps "recommends", "can mean", "only if", and both conditions. "Two things are true" matches the Lifeline's FAQ, which joins the conditions with "and when", so both are needed. If accepted, the next sentence (HELP-67) must change from "It reports" to "The Lifeline reports", or its source is no longer clear. "They" is kept from the original and means the counselor and the person. For the reviewer, not changed here: "safer plan" is the page's existing wording for the FAQ's "a less invasive plan for the caller/texter's safety". The Spanish page has a matching 46-word sentence.

- **Now (47 words):** The Lifeline's terms say that if a counselor believes there is an immediate threat to someone's physical safety, you may not get a say: they may ask for 911 or a mobile crisis team to come to your location, and can share your information without your permission.
  **Proposed:** The Lifeline's terms say that if a counselor believes there is an immediate threat to someone's physical safety, you may not get a say. In that case, the counselor may ask for 911 or a mobile crisis team to come to your location. In that case, your information can also be shared with them without your permission.
  **For the reviewer:** "You may not get a say" is word for word and stays in one sentence with its condition. On its own it would carry more weight, and notes/CONTENT-REVIEW.md already asks whether this wording could put someone off calling. "In that case" repeats so neither later sentence reads as a general rule. "Under the Lifeline's terms" replaces "The Lifeline's terms say" to avoid "say ... a say". One wording change for accuracy: "they ... can share" became "can also be shared". The terms give the 911 or mobile crisis request to the counselor, but the sharing clause says "We reserve the right to disclose". For the reviewer, not added because they would be new to this sentence: the terms describe a threat "requiring a rapid in-person response", and describe sharing with "authorities" such as 911, law enforcement, or mobile crisis teams, "in certain circumstances". The tool's rough estimate is still about grade 10, because words like "immediate" and "permission" stay. The Spanish page has a matching 52-word sentence.

- **Now (31 words):** A safety plan is a short written list of what to do when things get hard, made ahead of time so you don't have to work it out during a crisis.
  **Proposed:** A safety plan is a short written list of what to do when things get hard. It is made ahead of time, so you don't have to work out what to do during a crisis.
  **For the reviewer:** Uses "It is made" rather than "You make it": notes/CONTENT-REVIEW.md asks whether this description suits someone making a plan alone, and the next sentence says a professional can help. "Work it out" became "work out what to do" so "it" doesn't mean two things in one sentence. The Spanish page already splits this sentence the same way.

- **Now (28 words):** It can also help to tell an adult you trust, such as a parent or guardian, a relative, a teacher, a coach, a spiritual leader, or a doctor.
  **Proposed:** It can also help to tell an adult you trust. This could be someone like a parent or guardian, a relative, a teacher, a coach, a spiritual leader, or a doctor.
  **For the reviewer:** the same sentence is on teens/index.html, so change both or neither. All six examples are kept. "Someone like" keeps the list open, as "such as" does. The Spanish page has a matching 29-word sentence.

Whole page: this row and the next are the only flags, and a second pass over all visible text, footer included, found no others.

The question itself is unchanged. 'This could be' keeps the list as examples, as 'such as' did, and 'a trusted adult' replaces 'one' so the last sentence is clear on its own. Total words go up from 38 to 41. If accepted, the same words on teens/index.html (line 184, review packet row TEEN-61) and the parallel Spanish sentence (es/ayudar-a-alguien/index.html, line 210) need matching changes.
