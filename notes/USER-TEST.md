# Small user test plan (idea C6)

[[00 START HERE|Back to Start Here]]

**Goal:** see whether people can find help on the site quickly, before launch. This
tests the website, not the people, and not their mental health.

> [!warning] Run this only after the licensed reviewer has read this plan
> Testing a suicide prevention site can bring up hard feelings. The reviewer should
> confirm the safety steps below before anyone takes part.

## Who takes part

- 3 to 5 adults, 18 or older. Not minors, until supported ages and consent are decided.
- Not people who are currently struggling or in crisis. Ask everyone to skip
  taking part if the topic feels hard right now; no reason needed.
- At least one person who reads Spanish, and if possible one person who uses a
  screen reader or large text.

## Safety steps

1. Tell people before they agree: the site is about suicide prevention, they can
   stop at any time without saying why, and they won't be asked about their own life.
2. **Nobody calls, texts, or chats with a crisis line during the test.** For any
   task that ends at a call or text, they stop at the dial or compose screen and
   cancel. See [[SAFETY]] rule 7.
3. If anyone seems upset, stop the test, check in, and offer 988 (call or text) or
   911 in an emergency. Their wellbeing comes before finishing.
4. Collect no names, contact details, health information, or recordings in this
   repository. Notes use "Participant 1", "Participant 2". Keep any consent forms
   outside the project.

## Script

"Thanks for helping. We're testing a website, not you. There are no wrong answers.
Please think out loud as you go. All the situations are made up, and you won't be
asked about your own life. You can stop at any time. Please don't call, text, or
chat with any crisis line today: if a task ends at a phone or text screen, just tell
me and cancel."

## Tasks, on the person's own phone

Made-up situations. Start each one from the home page.

| # | Task | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Imagine you need to talk to someone right now. Show me how you'd do that." | Reaches the dial or text screen for 988, then cancels |
| 2 | "Imagine a friend sent you a worrying message. Find what the site suggests you do." | Finds Help Someone, the five steps or the urgent note |
| 3 | "Find out whether a counselor might call the police if you contact 988." | Finds "When emergency services get involved" on Get Help |
| 4 | Spanish reader: "Encuentra cómo enviar un mensaje de texto para recibir ayuda en español." | Finds "Envía AYUDA al 988" |
| 5 | "Imagine someone walks in and you want to leave this site fast." | Uses Quick exit |
| 6 | "Find something you could print to keep the number with you." | Reaches the wallet card page |
| 7 | "Imagine you're 16. Find what the site says about who might be told." | Finds the teen page or "If you're under 18" |

## What to note for each task

- Seconds to finish, or "gave up"
- Where they looked first, and anything they said was confusing
- Words they didn't understand

## Questions afterward

1. How did the site make you feel? Calm, rushed, confused, something else?
2. Was anything hard to find or hard to read?
3. Did anything feel wrong to say to someone who is struggling?
4. Did you trust it? Why or why not?
