Getting help for yourself

If you're struggling or having thoughts of suicide, you don't have to handle it alone. Here are ways to reach someone and what to expect.

The 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is free, confidential, and available any time. It is a separate service from Stillwater.

On a device that can't call or text? Call or text 988 from a phone, or go to 988lifeline.org. In immediate danger, call 911.

What happens when you reach out to 988

You don't need to be sure it's an emergency. The 988 Lifeline says anyone who is struggling can reach out, whether you are depressed, need to talk, or are thinking about suicide. It also takes concerns about alcohol or drug use.

If you call

You'll hear a short recorded message with options, such as pressing 1 for the Veterans Crisis Line or 2 for Spanish. Hold music plays while you are connected, so stay on the line. A counselor then says hello, asks if you are safe, listens to what's going on, and shares resources that may help.

If you text

Send any message to 988. Automated messages come first: you agree to the terms of service, answer a brief survey, and get a message asking you to wait. A counselor then says hello, asks if you are safe, and listens.

If you chat online

The chat opens with a short form. It asks for your zip code, whether you are having thoughts of suicide, and how upset you feel. A phone number is optional. You then wait to be connected to a counselor.

What it costs

Contacting 988 is free, and you don't need insurance. Your phone carrier may still charge for calls or texts if your plan doesn't include unlimited use.

If you can't get through

Try another way. If a call doesn't connect, try text or chat. If texting is busy, try a call or chat. In an emergency, call 911.

What stays private

The 988 Lifeline says conversations are confidential, which is not the same as anonymous. You don't have to say who you are or where you are. Counselors take notes, and call recordings or chat and text transcripts may be used to improve the service. The Lifeline's confidentiality statement lists when information can be shared, including when someone is at imminent risk of harm, when the law requires it, or when you agree.

On a shared phone or computer, visits to this site can show up in browser history, and calls or texts can show up in phone logs. Whether a call appears on a phone bill depends on the carrier.

When emergency services get involved

The 988 Lifeline is separate from 911. It says counselors contact emergency services only when someone is at imminent risk of harm and a less invasive plan for their safety can't be worked out with them. It reports that fewer than 2 in 100 calls involve emergency services, and that more than half of those happen with the caller's consent.

These details come from the 988 Lifeline's website and can change. Stillwater does not run 988 and cannot see who contacts it.

    For LGBTQ+ young people. Trained counselors by phone, text, or chat, free and confidential, 24/7. Message and data rates may apply to texts.

  • Crisis Text Line

    Trained volunteer crisis counselors by text, free and confidential, 24/7, anywhere in the United States. Message and data rates may apply.

    Text HOME to 741741

  • 911

    If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 911. Texting 911 only works in some places, and the FCC advises making a voice call if you can. If you text 911 where it isn't available, you should get an automatic message telling you to use another way.

Support over time

A crisis line can help with a hard moment. Longer-term care can help with what is behind it. The National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) says effective treatments are available for people at risk of suicide, including kinds of talk therapy such as cognitive behavioral therapy and dialectical behavior therapy. The American Foundation for Suicide Prevention says recovery is possible.

  • Talk to a health care provider. If you have a doctor or nurse you see, you can bring up your mental health yourself, and they can refer you to a specialist. NIMH has tips for that conversation.
  • Search for care near you. FindTreatment.gov is a confidential and anonymous treatment finder.
  • Call for a referral. SAMHSA's National Helpline, 1-800-662-4357, offers free treatment referrals and information, 24/7, in English and Spanish. It does not provide counseling and is not a crisis line.

Planning for a hard moment

A safety plan is a short written list of what to do when things get hard, made ahead of time so you don't have to work it out during a crisis. NIMH says research has shown that safety plans can help lower a person's risk of acting on suicidal thoughts. A therapist or other mental health professional can help you make one. A plan does not replace professional care.

A safety plan usually includes:

  • Your own warning signs that things are getting harder
  • Things you can do on your own to get through it
  • People and places that take your mind off it
  • Family or friends you can reach out to
  • Professionals and crisis lines you can contact
  • Ways to make your surroundings safer

You don't have to work through a list before asking for help. You can call or text 988, or call 911 in an emergency, at any point.

The developers of one widely studied plan offer the Stanley-Brown Safety Plan form (PDF) for personal use. Anything you write on it stays with you. Stillwater does not see or save it.

If you're under 18

You can contact these services yourself. The 988 Lifeline's terms set no age requirement, and you don't have to give your name or age to get support. It can help to save 988 in your phone now, in case you need it later.

It can also help to tell an adult you trust, such as a parent or guardian, a relative, a teacher, a school counselor, a coach, a spiritual leader, or a doctor.

Conversations are confidential, with limits. The 988 Lifeline says it may share information if someone is at imminent risk of harm, or if a minor is being abused or neglected as state law defines it. School counselors and other school staff may need to tell a parent or guardian if they are worried about your safety. It's okay to ask what someone can keep private.

About Stillwater

Stillwater is an early project to help people in the United States find suicide prevention support. Stillwater is not a crisis service. The call, text, and chat links on this site connect you to outside services, such as the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 911.
